01 — Overview

Every lane is a queue. Every emergency is a priority.

Fixed-timer traffic signals treat every lane the same, whether it has two cars or twenty. This project replaces that fixed timer with a queue-driven controller: the busiest, most urgent lane gets served next — not just whichever lane is due.

Each approach to the intersection (North, East, South, West) is modelled as a FIFO queue of arriving vehicles. Vehicles enter at the back and are cleared from the front, in the exact order they arrived — the same discipline real traffic follows.

Sitting above the four queues is a priority queue that decides which lane's signal turns green next. Under normal conditions, priority is driven by queue length — the more congested lane waits less. The moment an emergency vehicle joins any queue, it is pushed to the front of its lane and its lane is bumped to the top of the priority queue, overriding the normal rotation.

The result is a controller that stays fair under everyday traffic, but reacts instantly when it matters.

02 — Features

What the controller actually does

Six behaviours, each backed directly by a queue or priority-queue operation — nothing here needs anything heavier.

🟢

Adaptive Green Time

Green duration scales with how many vehicles are queued, so a full lane isn't cut off mid-flow and an empty one isn't held open.

🚑

Emergency Override

Ambulances and fire trucks are inserted at the front of their lane's queue and immediately promoted in the priority queue.

⏱️

Starvation Guard

A waiting-time weight is folded into every lane's priority score, so a low-traffic lane can never be skipped forever.

🧭

N-Way Junctions

The lane-as-queue model isn't fixed to four arms — add a queue and a priority entry, and a 3-way or 5-way junction works the same way.

📊

Live Queue Metrics

Per-lane queue length, wait time, and clear rate are tracked in real time, so the scheduling decision is always visible, not a black box.

🧮

O(log n) Scheduling

The priority queue is backed by a binary heap, so re-ranking four (or forty) lanes after every arrival stays fast.

01

Enqueue on arrival

Every new car calls enqueue() on its lane's queue. Ambulances call enqueueFront() instead, jumping every waiting car in that lane.

02

Rank lanes by priority

Before each cycle, all four lanes are pushed into a priority queue keyed by queue length — with any lane holding an ambulance forced to the top.

03

Dequeue on green

The top-priority lane turns green and calls dequeue(), clearing vehicles front-to-back until its turn ends, then the cycle repeats.
